Early Market Regime Forecasting

Can a market-state transition be forecast before it begins—and can that warning improve a 21-day crypto-perpetual swing process after costs?

Scientific research monograph, evidence review, and preregistrable experiment design. Repository snapshot: v8 e971daf; Kepler fbdc8b3. Literature and project sources checked for this review through 14 October 2026.

Independent judgment. The question is worth one small, tightly preregistered forecasting experiment. Current evidence does not establish that crypto-perpetual regimes can be forecast before onset, and it does not establish a profitable 1–21 day strategy. The strongest prior explanation for an apparent warning is ordinary momentum or early detection after a move has already started. The first experiment must test transition-onset prediction separately from continuation, volatility forecasting, and trading value.

1. Abstract and verdict

This review asks whether observable price, volatility, volume, and accessible derivatives information can forecast the onset of a new market regime before that regime is visible in a causal state filter. It distinguishes that task from (i) noticing a transition once it has begun or forecasting continuation, and (ii) forecasting future volatility. These are different targets with different labels, baselines, and economic interpretations.

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Regime-switching models provide probabilistic state inference; change-point algorithms estimate whether a distribution has already changed; time-series momentum has a credible but horizon- and sample-dependent empirical record; and realized-volatility models can forecast risk. None of these results, by itself, demonstrates advance prediction of a new directional regime in crypto perpetuals. The critical-transition literature provides candidate precursors such as rising autocorrelation and variance, but its mechanism requires assumptions that are not generally guaranteed in markets, and realistic false-alarm rates can be severe.

PROJECT_EVIDENCE Kepler has substantial research on momentum, risk, and technical hypotheses, but no direct H1-A transition-onset forecast test was identified in the reviewed reports. Existing results are mixed: some trend/momentum variants are weakly positive, several alternatives are NO_EDGE, a prior ensemble's full-period result weakens in its later segment, and the project itself marks all historical development data as seen. The project’s reported G3 boundary is 1 April 2026; its backtest specification says that period was not downloaded and is reserved for a single user-commanded evaluation.

DESIGN_INFERENCE The smallest useful first test is a low-dimensional, daily, event-hazard forecast of a prespecified persistent-trend onset, evaluated against a strong recent-return baseline in purged rolling-origin tests. It should use 4-hour data only as a later incremental feature block. No GPU, large neural model, or new backtest engine is justified. The historical run is a feasibility study, not independent proof; final confirmation must use a model frozen before any sealed or genuinely prospective observations are inspected.

Verdict in one sentence: H1-A is scientifically interesting and cheap to test, but should be treated as unproven and probably difficult; reject a claim of useful transition forecasting if a frozen model cannot materially improve calibrated event forecasts over momentum before onset, and reject trading utility if that forecast advantage does not survive realistic costs and a risk-matched momentum control.

2. Question, hypotheses, and evidence labels

A “regime” is not directly observed. It is a model-dependent summary of a return-generating process. A statistical regime label may be useful for description or risk management without being a forecast target, and a profitable directional rule may work without there being a discrete latent state. The experiment therefore tests one operational event definition, not the metaphysical claim that markets contain a fixed number of natural states.

Three distinct questions. A result in one row cannot be used as proof of another.

4. What the literature does and does not establish

4.1 Regime inference is not transition anticipation

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Hamilton’s Markov-switching framework allows autoregressive parameters to change when an unobserved discrete state changes; filtered probabilities are based on information available up to time t, while smoothed probabilities condition on the full sample and therefore use future observations. Filardo’s time-varying transition-probability extension makes transition probabilities depend on observed covariates. These are principled ways to estimate a state or conditional state-switching probability, but evidence of state persistence or a well-fitting switching model is not itself evidence of useful pre-onset forecasts.

A causal HMM can produce P(St=k | Ft). To forecast a transition it needs P(St+h≠St | Ft), which combines filtered state probabilities, a transition matrix, duration assumptions, and covariates. A visually convincing retrospectively smoothed state chart can be severely misleading if its labels move after future data are added. Any HMM result must report the online filtered distribution, not a smoothed path masquerading as a real-time forecast.

4.2 Change-point detection mainly answers “has it changed?”

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Bayesian online change-point detection (BOCPD) recursively updates a posterior over the run length since the latest change. CUSUM, Page–Hinkley, and related detectors look for evidence that observations have departed from a prior distribution. These methods are useful for H1-B: detecting a shift with a quantifiable delay. When the change time is stochastic and no observable precursor shifts the hazard, a detector cannot reliably warn before the first evidence of change. Calling its posterior “early forecasting” confuses online recognition at or after onset with prediction before onset.

Standard HMMs also impose geometric state durations because a constant transition probability makes the probability of leaving a state independent of its age. Hidden semi-Markov models and duration-dependent Markov models can relax this assumption. They may improve duration modelling, but a duration model is predictive only if the age dependence is stable and calibrated out of sample; fitting an attractive duration curve after seeing the sample does not prove tradable lead time.

4.3 Critical-transition warnings are conditional theories, not universal market laws

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Early-warning research on slowly driven systems derives possible signs of critical slowing down: slower recovery after perturbations, rising lag-1 autocorrelation, and rising variance as resilience declines near a bifurcation. The theory is useful when a system approaches a known type of critical threshold. But financial returns are heavy-tailed, exposed to jumps and external news, and do not generally evolve as a slowly forced system near a stable equilibrium. A volatility squeeze followed by a jump may be a market pattern, but that observation does not establish the critical-slowing-down mechanism.

Methods papers explicitly warn that real-time early-warning reliability must be measured with controls and false alarms. Boettiger and Hastings show that common summary indicators can have substantial false-alarm and missed-event rates even in favorable settings; later reviews emphasize that not every regime shift exhibits critical slowing down and that detection is statistically hard in short noisy records. A 2022 crypto preprint clusters four market states from evolving cross-asset correlations over 2017–2022; it is a useful candidate feature study, not proof that warnings preceded an operational onset or generated net returns. A 2025 PLOS One paper proposes a heteroskedastic-network warning method for one S&P 500 series; it is an adjacent risk-warning result, not a multi-asset crypto-perpetual trading validation.

4.4 Trend and momentum are both the strongest baseline and a confounder

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Time-series momentum has been documented across diversified futures markets over monthly horizons; cryptocurrency studies have reported weekly or shorter effects, but the findings depend on sample, universe, weighting, horizon, and transaction feasibility. Liu and Tsyvinski report cryptocurrency-specific return predictability, including time-series momentum on 1–4 week horizons, in their sample. Other cryptocurrency momentum studies report weak or contradictory results. These observations justify a strong momentum comparator; they do not imply that momentum predicts the beginning of a new regime.

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED A 2025 peer-reviewed cryptocurrency momentum study uses weekly winner-minus-loser portfolios across a changing top-30 market-cap universe from 2016–2023. Its central result concerns cross-sectional momentum and a severe portfolio crash from one token; that design is not the same as same-asset time-series momentum, transition forecasting, or perpetual-futures execution. The distinction matters: apparent agreement across “momentum” studies may disappear when target construction is checked.

DESIGN_INFERENCE If an onset label is built from a future 20-day return and path efficiency, lagged return and path efficiency are mechanically close to the label. The comparison must therefore include them in the strongest baseline. A squeeze, funding anomaly, or volume feature only supports H1-A if it predicts later confirmed onset incrementally beyond those lags. Otherwise it is momentum arriving early, not additional transition information.

4.5 EMA, RSI, breakouts, levels, volume, and funding

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Technical-analysis research includes carefully tested patterns and support/resistance rules, and some market-specific studies find conditional price behaviour around published levels. Other reviews warn that data-snooping, changing market structure, and transaction costs make profitability difficult to establish. EMA and RSI are deterministic transforms of lagged prices; a 20/50 EMA cross does not add independent information merely because it has a familiar name. Breakout and support/resistance features can still add value, but only in a nested out-of-sample comparison with their underlying recent returns and range measures.

PROJECT_EVIDENCE Kepler’s prior experiments show why that control matters: trend-family results overlap; some candidate patterns fail net of costs; technical hypotheses with no direct event-onset target do not prove early regime forecasting. Volume from hourly bars is not order-book depth. Funding is a periodic transfer linked to perp/spot basis and positioning pressure; it may forecast returns, volatility, or neither. A one-venue funding series without OI, liquidation prints, or order-book depth cannot identify which mechanism is operating.

4.6 Volatility forecasts can help risk without forecasting direction

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Realized-volatility models such as HAR-RV aggregate daily, weekly, and monthly realized variance to forecast future volatility. GARCH, stochastic-volatility, and range-based methods provide other volatility baselines. This can improve position sizing or reduce tail exposure. Such a result is H1-C unless the same model also demonstrates pre-onset directional-state prediction. Volatility compression is a risk/dispersion statistic, not a directional label.

4.7 Crypto perpetual results do not transfer automatically

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED Perpetual futures are anchored to spot/index prices through funding transfers; they do not have a conventional expiry and their basis/funding dynamics vary by venue. A 2023 cryptocurrency-futures paper finds basis has stronger cross-sectional predictive evidence than its momentum proxy, and its return predictability changes with holding horizon. That is a useful warning about contract-specific predictors, but it studies cross-sectional futures returns rather than a 21-day BTCUSDT perpetual transition model. The evidence cannot be imported as a universal perp alpha.

UNKNOWN No reviewed primary study directly demonstrates that OHLCV + funding reliably anticipates a persistent directional transition 1–7 days before onset in Binance-style crypto perpetuals, then improves a costed long/short swing process held at most 21 days. This is the empirical gap the proposed test addresses.

6. Operational regime and onset definition

DESIGN_INFERENCE No short monograph can establish a unique natural taxonomy of market states. The first experiment should use one simple, scale-normalized label and make the label rule immutable before fitting. It tests the narrow claim “a new persistent directional episode is forecast before its first threshold-crossing day.” It does not claim to forecast every bull/bear/range/volatility state.

6.1 Daily causal state

For instrument i and UTC day t, let ri,t = ln(Ci,t/Ci,t−1), computed from completed daily last-trade OHLCV bars. Define a 20-day standardized drift and path efficiency:

Z20(i,t) = Σ(j=0…19) r(i,t−j) / √[Σ(j=0…19) r(i,t−j)² + ε]
ER20(i,t) = |Σ(j=0…19) r(i,t−j)| / [Σ(j=0…19) |r(i,t−j)| + ε]
q(i,t) = +1 if Z20 ≥ 1.25 and ER20 ≥ 0.35; −1 if Z20 ≤ −1.25 and ER20 ≥ 0.35; otherwise 0.

q=+1 and q=−1 are operational up- and down-trend states; q=0 is non-trend/range. Use fixed thresholds; do not optimize them on the evaluation period. The exact thresholds are a design choice, not a literature fact. A small, declared sensitivity check may later vary one threshold at a time, but it cannot replace or redefine the primary label.

6.2 Candidate onset, confirmation, and forecast target

  • A candidate onset t₀ is the first day q changes from 0 to +1 or −1 after at least 10 consecutive q=0 days. This re-arm rule prevents one long trend from being counted as many new transitions.
  • The candidate is a confirmed transition only if the same signed state appears on at least 8 of the next 10 completed daily closes. Confirmation date is c=t₀+10. A failed candidate is not an event; it is recorded as an unconfirmed crossing for false-alarm analysis.
  • At forecast origin t, H1-A target is whether a confirmed onset occurs on any day t+1…t+7, with direction up/down. The feature vector may use data available through close t only. Training rows are not admitted until their full target and confirmation window are observable.
  • H1-B is measured separately: time from onset t₀ to first causal detection. Predictions first raised at or after t₀ cannot score as H1-A.
  • H1-C uses next-5/next-10-day realized variance as a distinct target and reports volatility loss and risk utility only.

Illustrative case, not a result. Suppose a BTC series has q=0 through Monday, crosses to q=+1 on Tuesday, and satisfies the 8-of-10 persistence rule. A warning raised the previous Friday with elevated forecast probability is a four-day H1-A lead. A warning first raised Wednesday after the onset is an H1-B detection. If the same rule labels Tuesday retrospectively using the next ten closes, no future close may be included in Friday’s inputs.

7. Smallest falsifiable experiment

7.1 Primary question and estimand

DESIGN_INFERENCE Estimate whether a small feature block improves the probability forecast of confirmed up/down trend onset within seven days beyond (a) a state-conditioned base rate and (b) lagged momentum, efficiency, and volatility. Only after that forecast gate passes should the project map probabilities into a shadow position rule and test after-cost economics. This staged design keeps the first decision cheap and prevents an attractive P&L from concealing that the model merely reacts after onset.

pᵈ(i,t) = P(confirmed onset with direction d ∈ {up, down} in t+1…t+7 | Xᵢ,≤t)
Primary increment: out-of-sample forecast skill(M2) − forecast skill(M1)

7.2 Data and instruments

  • Historical feasibility panel: Kepler hourly OHLCV, mark, and funding sources, aggregated from completed bars to UTC daily features. Use only dates before 2026-04-01. Prefer an as-of tradable universe and the project’s usable/divergence cutoffs. If only the fixed extended-9 universe can be reconstructed, say explicitly that it is survivor-biased and do not generalize to all crypto.
  • Core replication slice: report BTC and ETH separately even if a broader panel is pooled. Pool asset-level observations only with symbol effects and inference clustered by calendar date; coins share large common shocks, so nine symbols are not nine independent markets.
  • Frequency: daily closes are primary because the target and holding window are daily and effective sample size is event episodes, not bar count. 4H aggregates are a predeclared incremental feature ablation only if the daily model passes its event-count gate. Do not count four 4H bars as four independent daily trials.
  • Point-in-time features: daily volume and funding must be timestamped as known at the forecast origin. No future fill, revised symbol list, or forward-filled stale funding across missing intervals. Report all rejected days, missingness, and symbol-specific coverage.

7.4 Forecast splits and leakage controls

  1. Start with at least three calendar years of training, then use contiguous six-month rolling-origin test windows, expanding the training history each time. No random train/test shuffle.
  2. All transformations, normalizers, feature selection, regularization, probability calibration, and alert threshold selection are fitted within the training fold only.
  3. Because a label at origin t needs up to seven days to the onset and ten days to confirmation, purge at least 17 days around forecast fold boundaries. For the economic evaluation, purge/embargo 40 days to cover the forecast horizon, confirmation, and maximum 21-day holding overlap.
  4. Do not allow training observations whose outcome has not fully matured by the training cutoff. Never use a full-sample HMM smoother, centered rolling statistic, later universe membership, or post-event normalization.
  5. Run future-corruption tests: perturb or replace all data after randomly selected cutoffs; every past feature and prediction must remain byte-identical. Independently shift each feature stream and check timestamp alignment, funding settlement timing, and daily-bar close construction.
  6. Freeze a protocol hash, data manifest, feature code hash, and model specification before any sealed or prospective evaluation. The current G3 interval remains untouched.

8. Validation, statistical risk, and stopping rules

8.1 Effective sample size and uncertainty

Daily rows overlap in their seven-day targets. Assets also move together. Ordinary IID standard errors would overstate precision. The primary uncertainty interval should use a moving-block or stationary bootstrap over contiguous calendar-time blocks (minimum 28 days), resampling all assets together; event-level summaries should additionally cluster events that occur within a shared ±14-day market episode. Report raw counts of confirmed up/down events, unique date clusters, and events per outer fold. Cap the model and search space before looking at validation results.

DESIGN_INFERENCE Require at least 30 confirmed up events and 30 confirmed down events across all outer-test periods, with at least five non-empty test windows. This is a feasibility floor, not a guarantee of power. If it is not met, label the result INCONCLUSIVE—INSUFFICIENT EVENTS; do not soften the state threshold, add more assets after seeing performance, or substitute volatility events.

8.2 Multiplicity and selection

Historical Kepler dates have been used in many searches and model decisions. A new label does not reset that exposure. Treat the historical rolling-origin result as a development diagnostic. The H1-A confirmatory claim requires a frozen model on untouched post-boundary or genuinely prospective observations with enough independent event clusters. Record every attempted label, feature, horizon, and model. Use a family-wise correction for the limited prespecified secondary horizons and robustness checks; report a Deflated Sharpe Ratio or PBO only for economic strategy selection, not as a substitute for an untouched test.

Do not perform a large grid over regime thresholds, smoothing windows, feature sets, or probability cutoffs. If model M2 fails, the correct action is to report failure or an underpowered test, not to search variants until one crosses 0.05. A positive result on one event definition supports only that operational target.

9. Economic value and execution costs

Forecast accuracy is the first gate; economic value is a separate second gate. If the forecast gate passes, evaluate one frozen, simple shadow mapping only—do not launch a broad strategy search. When the estimated up- or down-transition probability crosses the training-selected alert threshold, enter at the next eligible daily open in the predicted direction, hold at most 21 days, and do not pyramid. Position risk is set using the same lagged volatility target and cap for every comparison. The probability threshold is fixed to the false-alarm budget, not optimized for backtest profit.

Compare the shadow exposure with: (1) cash/no position; (2) same-asset 21-day simple momentum using the same sizing, entry timing, and exits; (3) a momentum plus volatility-targeting-only control; and (4) a passive market benchmark. The key economic quantity is the net difference from the matched momentum control, not the absolute Sharpe. Report net P&L, Sharpe, drawdown, expected shortfall, turnover, funding paid/received, fee, slippage, exposure, and performance by direction and market episode. Do not call a lower drawdown “alpha” if returns merely fell because exposure fell.

13. Final scientific judgment

Is H1-A worth researching?

Yes, once. The gap is real: most regime labels and change-point methods answer what state the market is in or whether it has changed; they do not establish that the next state can be forecast ahead of onset. OHLCV and funding are cheap enough to test. But the prior probability of a robust, tradable, incremental warning should be modest. Markets do not have to approach a smooth tipping point, alerts may be late, and any gain can be ordinary momentum or a risk filter.

What is the strongest alternative explanation?

The most likely false positive is momentum-conditioned relabeling: the future “trend regime” is defined by realized return persistence, and predictors near its boundary naturally resemble the same trailing return and efficiency used to define it. A model may appear to forecast a transition when it merely recognizes the early part of a move. The mandatory comparison is therefore M2 against a well-tuned but low-dimensional M1 momentum baseline, with onset and confirmation kept separate. A second alternative is that the model only predicts volatility and reduces exposure; that is useful if true, but it is H1-C, not H1-A.

What should the first experiment be?

Pre-register the fixed daily state/event definition in §6; perform a read-only event-count and data-quality feasibility audit; then fit only M0, M1, and M2 on historical pre-2026-04 data with expanding walk-forward folds, 17-day forecast purging, and 40-day economic embargo. Do not touch G3. Treat those historical results as development evidence. If and only if forecast skill passes the event-count, calibration, lead-time, and false-alarm gates, freeze the model and plan a separate prospective evaluation. Only after that can a single 21-day capped shadow mapping be compared with risk-matched momentum after costs.

How much compute and when should the hypothesis be rejected?

The forecast stage is CPU-small: likely seconds to minutes for the fixed feature panel, plus a few minutes for block bootstrap. Most cost is in validating the tape, event boundaries, and executable assumptions. Reject a practically useful signal for this specification if M2 does not achieve at least a five-percent proper-score improvement over M1 with a one-sided 95% block-bootstrap interval above zero, or if the alert budget/lead-time gate fails. If events are too few, report inconclusive rather than “no effect.” If forecast skill passes but net risk-matched 21-day returns do not improve after fees, slippage, and funding, conclude that a forecast may exist but does not add economic value for this swing process.

Final answer. H1-A deserves a small falsification experiment, not a strategy build. The first result to seek is honest predictive information before onset beyond momentum. The first result to distrust is a full-sample Sharpe, a smoothed HMM chart, a volatility win, or an alert that only fires after the transition is already underway.
