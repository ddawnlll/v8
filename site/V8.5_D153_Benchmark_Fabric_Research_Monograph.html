Document status and reading rule

D-153 RESEARCH MONOGRAPH / PROPOSED BENCHMARK CONSTITUTION / NO NEW ECONOMIC CLAIM. This document is a research and architecture proposal for a V8.5 Benchmark Fabric. It extends, but does not replace, the existing V8/V8.5 Assurance Fabric, three-Oracle architecture, DataRoleLedger, Statistical Court, Market World Foundry, System Proving Ground, continuous evidence succession, and D-152 scenario-centric evidence profile. Nothing in this document promotes a strategy, certifies profitability, converts synthetic evidence into economic authority, or turns a diagnostic score into a deployment permission.

The reading rule is deliberately strict. Capability measurement, evidence authority, economic projection, and deployment readiness are different objects. A number may be useful for comparing two frozen policy versions while being legally incapable of supporting a profitability claim. Conversely, an admissible economic observation may be too sparse to justify a stable benchmark rank. The Benchmark Fabric exists to keep those distinctions explicit rather than to erase them behind a single dashboard number.

The document inherits the project's epistemic vocabulary: LITERATURE_SUPPORTED PROJECT_EVIDENCE_SUPPORTED DESIGN_INFERENCE PROVISIONAL_DECISION LOCKED_INVARIANT OPEN_QUESTION REJECTED_OPTION. Labels apply to propositions, not to the attractiveness of a chart.

Compatibility rule. If any proposal below conflicts with an active constitutional invariant, the active invariant wins. In particular: no scalar score may certify production readiness; hard defeaters are non-compensable; synthetic PASS cannot establish SUPPORTED_EDGE; and a consumed holdout cannot be made pristine again by renaming it.

Abstract

V8 currently possesses substantial evaluation machinery but lacks a single, coherent benchmark product that answers a simpler operational question: is version N+1 genuinely better than version N across a sufficiently broad and independently governed test population? The historical 12-month quad run is useful as a diagnostic court, but repeated tuning against the same realized tape makes its headline return unsuitable as the main measure of progress. Deriving more metrics—Oracle recall, fee retention, MFE retention, precision—from the same tape improves diagnosis but does not create independent evidence.

This monograph proposes the V8.5 Benchmark Fabric: a versioned external-evaluation harness that evaluates a frozen policy against a battery of real, resampled, synthetic, adversarial, execution-reference, and prospective populations; produces a multi-dimensional capability profile; preserves non-compensable hard gates; and, only when economically admissible evidence exists, emits a separately typed capital-outcome projection. It deliberately borrows commodity methods and software where doing so reduces implementation and validation risk: purged/walk-forward/CPCV splitting from mature portfolio-research libraries; independent execution replay through LEAN where contracts can be matched; standardized reporting concepts from Qlib, TradeMaster, FinRL-Meta and PRUDEX-Compass; and established anti-overfitting methods such as White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA, Deflated Sharpe Ratio and PBO/CSCV.

The central design is therefore not a new backtester. It is an adapter, manifest, measurement, and authority system. V8 retains the domain-specific objects that external packages cannot supply: Opportunity sovereignty, Target/Hindsight/Parity Oracles, Expert habitats and witness semantics, Recoverable Regret, Market World Foundry, minimal defeater search, claim authority, evidence lineage, Kaizen succession, and promotion governance. External engines are treated as independent instruments, not sovereign truth sources.

A diagnostic CapabilityScore ∈ [0,100] is allowed for regression tracking and research prioritization, but it is explicitly separated from the GateVector, ProductionEvidenceCertificate, and CapitalOutcomeProjection. This permits a human operator to see “benchmark 68/100, +7.2 vs incumbent” while simultaneously seeing “G5 statistical court BLOCKED; G6 protected OOS NOT_RUN; no forward $1000 projection authorized.” The scalar helps answer how much did the system improve?; the gate vector answers what are we allowed to believe?

Baseline: what V8 has today and why it is not yet a benchmark

PROJECT_EVIDENCE_SUPPORTED. The current V8 evidence architecture already contains most of the components needed for a serious benchmark: immutable EvaluationCase identity, AuthorityProjection, claim rules, provenance and common-mode graphs, hard defeaters, continuous evidence epochs, a revocable non-scalar ProductionEvidenceCertificate, D-152 PolicyEvidenceProfile, a Market World Foundry, System Proving Ground, DataRoleLedger, and TEVV probes. The missing object is the orchestrated benchmark population and its comparison contract.

D-152 correctly demoted the 2025-07-01 → 2026-06-30 quad to BURNED_DIAGNOSTIC. That run remains valuable: it exposes fee drag, symbol concentration, candidate density, opportunity recall, and execution pathology. It is no longer allowed to masquerade as an untouched judge of the policy lineage that was repeatedly developed against it.

The core mistake would be to produce ten more metrics from the same trajectory and call the result ten independent pieces of evidence. They are different views of one evidence source. The Benchmark Fabric therefore counts evaluation populations and their dependency structure, not merely metric count.

The benchmark problem

1. A benchmark is a measurement system, not a score formula

A benchmark requires at least five frozen objects: a subject, a population, a contract, a measurement plan, and a comparison rule. Without these, a score is a moving target. Changing transaction costs, symbol universe, regime labels, path-generation rules, or metric normalization can make version N+1 appear better while the policy itself is unchanged.

BenchmarkCase = PolicyIdentity × PopulationManifest × EconomicContract × MeasurementPlan × ComparatorSet

2. The evaluator can itself be overfit

Once Kaizen sees the benchmark repeatedly, the benchmark becomes part of the training environment. A fixed public battery can eventually be gamed even if its data are synthetic. Therefore benchmark evolution needs the same lineage discipline as policy evolution: development populations may be visible; qualification populations are sealed; novelty populations are generated or revealed after policy freeze; evaluator changes create new benchmark versions and cannot silently rewrite historical ranks.

3. No single population answers all questions

Historical replay has genuine market geometry but limited path coverage. Synthetic worlds can cover mechanisms never observed in the historical window but do not carry empirical frequencies. CPCV can expose path dependence under real samples but does not create truly new market history. A second execution engine can falsify implementation assumptions but does not prove alpha. Prospective shadow is strongest for temporal generalization but accumulates slowly. A serious benchmark composes these as typed evidence rather than pretending one can replace another.

Most benchmark pathologies are ontological before they are statistical: using Hindsight Oracle value as a selectable strategy target; treating synthetic frequency as future probability; treating a score normalization as an estimate of readiness; or treating several metrics from one tape as several independent experiments. The architecture must make those mistakes difficult at the type level.

Epistemic separation: observation, inference, projection and authority

Every benchmark datum is annotated by epistemic type. A historical ledger value is an observation under a simulator. A bootstrap interval is an inference conditional on a resampling scheme. A synthetic stress result is a model-conditional counterexample. A one-year capital distribution is a projection conditional on an admissible evidence population and aggregation model. None automatically becomes a claim about the live future.

OBSERVATION
    "On protected OOS case X, frozen policy P returned +8.1% after declared costs."

INFERENCE
    "Across declared dependent OOS folds, the median net utility is positive and
     the confidence procedure excludes the preregistered null under its assumptions."

MODEL-CONDITIONAL STRESS
    "A passport-passing liquidity/correlation world at distance d causes 24% MaxDD."

PROJECTION
    "Given the calibrated projection population and its weighting assumptions,
     the P50 one-year terminal capital is $1,180."

AUTHORITY
    "The current AssuranceCase permits ECONOMIC_REPLICATION but not LIVE_SUPPORTED."

These statements have different falsifiers. The benchmark report must preserve that structure all the way to the renderer.

Normative requirements for D-153

  1. Version everything that can change a benchmark result: population, metrics, weights, thresholds, normalization, execution adapter, regime classifier, Oracle contract, cost model, aggregation rule, and projection model.
  2. Freeze before protected evaluation. Policy and benchmark case manifests are sealed before any protected result is read.
  3. Count research debt. Automatically generated, discarded, failed, and unpublished challengers remain trials.
  4. Preserve dependency. Ten metrics from one tape are not ten experiments; 20 overlapping folds are not 20 IID observations.
  5. No hard-gate compensation. PIT, ledger, causal, authority, statistical, OOS, shadow and live gates remain separate.
  6. Use external tools as instruments. LEAN/skfolio/vectorbt/Qlib outputs receive contracts and adapters; they do not gain V8 authority.
  7. Projection is optional. If admissible economic populations are insufficient, the correct output is UNKNOWN.
  8. Benchmark improvement must be attributable. A challenger delta is decomposed by discovery, selection, risk, execution, generalization and failure topology, not just total score.
  9. Benchmark evolution must not erase history. A new evaluator version creates a new benchmark series; bridge studies may link versions but may not pretend identical semantics.
  10. Every positive result carries a falsifier. Reports must state what observation would materially weaken the conclusion.

PRUDEX-Compass: precedent for multi-axis evaluation

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED PRUDEX-Compass was proposed specifically because profit-only evaluation of financial reinforcement-learning systems is inadequate. The framework evaluates eight FinRL methods on four long-term real-world datasets across six perspectives—Profitability, Risk-control, Universality, Diversity, Reliability and Explainability—using seventeen measures. This is close to the conceptual problem V8 faces: a system can look attractive on return while failing universality, risk, or reliability.

V8 should transfer the separation of dimensions, not the exact PRUDEX normalizations. A benchmark domain is useful because it keeps failure visible. Once domains are collapsed to one score, the normalization and weighting scheme become an additional research choice. Therefore D-153 uses PRUDEX as evidence that multi-axis evaluation is a serious benchmark design family, while rejecting any claim that its particular axes or weights are universally correct for crypto swing trading.

TradeMaster: benchmark as standardized laboratory

TradeMaster's useful contribution is organizational rather than ontological. It treats dataset preparation, trading environment, algorithm implementation, benchmark execution and reporting as one reproducible platform. This matters because many published comparisons are not true comparisons: transaction-cost assumptions, preprocessing, rebalance schedules, reward definitions, and market windows differ.

For V8, the transferable principle is all-else-equal evaluation. A challenger should not be compared with an incumbent under different data semantics or execution physics. Every BenchmarkCase therefore pins the information contract, costs, capital, allocator, simulator level, population and metric definitions. External tools are invoked only through adapters that produce a canonical V8 receipt.

BAD COMPARISON
    incumbent @ V8 simulator / fee model A / 4 symbols
    challenger @ external engine / fee model B / 10 symbols

GOOD COMPARISON
    same FrozenDecisionStream
    same capital
    same symbol universe
    same timestamps

    same allowed execution semantics

TradeMaster is not evidence that RL is appropriate for V8, nor that its metric set defines economic truth. It is evidence that a benchmark becomes substantially more credible when data, environment and evaluation are packaged as a repeatable system.

Source: TradeMaster, NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks track.

FinRL-Meta: environment multiplicity and DataOps

FinRL-Meta explicitly motivates a data-centric infrastructure for financial reinforcement learning around low signal-to-noise, survivorship bias and backtest overfitting. It provides a universe of market environments instead of treating one dataset as the entire research world. For D-153 the relevant lesson is not “use FinRL”; it is that evaluation breadth belongs in infrastructure, not in an ad-hoc analyst notebook.

V8 goes further by attaching authority and dependency to each environment. Ten correlated slices of the same year are not counted as ten independent worlds. A synthetic world and a protected real OOS world are not interchangeable. A market environment is therefore represented as a typed EvaluationPopulation with lineage, dependence and admissibility.

Source: Liu et al., “FinRL-Meta: Market Environments and Benchmarks for Data-Driven Financial Reinforcement Learning,” arXiv:2211.03107.

Qlib: repeated-seed reporting and model/portfolio separation

Microsoft Qlib provides a broad quantitative-research platform and benchmark conventions that distinguish prediction diagnostics (IC, ICIR, Rank IC) from portfolio outcomes (annualized return, information ratio, maximum drawdown). Its public benchmark examples report mean and standard deviation over repeated random seeds for model results. The transferable point is that a benchmark should expose dispersion and repeatability, not just the best run.

V8 is mostly deterministic at the policy/runtime layer, so “seed variability” means something different. Randomness enters through synthetic generation, bootstrap populations, randomized subset/time-window evaluation, or stochastic external models. Those seeds are therefore benchmark inputs, and the report shows dispersion across them. Deterministic replays should instead be byte-identical; variance there is a defect, not uncertainty.

Multi-OOS evaluation: one future slice is not enough for research comparison

Several quantitative studies evaluate one fixed policy/hyperparameter family across many out-of-sample setups rather than presenting a single favorable test period. One high-dimensional portfolio study evaluates deep-RL approaches across 48 U.S. equity portfolio setups, varying portfolio size, stock-selection criteria and transaction-cost assumptions while retaining a common hyperparameter setup. The important transfer is the design logic: hold the policy class fixed while varying the environment dimensions that could expose fragility.

V8's equivalent is not “test every coin and average the return.” The deployment selector is allowed to abstain and concentrate. The benchmark instead asks whether the decision process remains lawful and useful as universe, chronology, cost and regime vary. A newly added weak asset should not mechanically create a trade; the ability to say NO_TRADE is part of generalization.

Source: “High-Dimensional Stock Portfolio Trading with Deep Reinforcement Learning,” arXiv:2112.04755.

Purging, embargo and combinatorial out-of-sample paths

Time-series trading labels and campaigns overlap. Random IID cross-validation can leak information because training observations may share future windows, labels, or economic episodes with the test set. Purging removes training observations whose information overlaps test observations; embargo inserts a time buffer; combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV/CSCV-family designs) creates multiple out-of-sample paths instead of a single split.

For V8, CPCV has a narrow but valuable role: it estimates path/ranking sensitivity inside already observed real history. It does not manufacture untouched future evidence. Once the historical tape has influenced policy development, CPCV over that tape remains diagnostic. This distinction is constitutional:

CPCV(BURNED_DIAGNOSTIC)
    → useful distributional diagnostic
    → can expose instability / rank reversal
    → cannot restore POLICY_FROZEN_OOS authority

CPCV(PROTECTED population, preregistered)
    → may contribute to protected evaluation
    → still carries dependence structure
    → does not equal N independent futures

skfolio provides a practical implementation of CombinatorialPurgedCV, with purging/embargo and multiple test paths; its cross-validation tooling can return a population of multi-period portfolios. VectorBT Pro exposes related purged and combinatorial splitters for high-throughput research. D-153 should use or validate such commodity primitives rather than casually reimplement them.

Sources: skfolio CPCV documentation; Bailey et al., Probability of Backtest Overfitting.

Regime-conditional evaluation: preserve the shape of failure

A 2026 regime-conditional portfolio-evaluation study evaluates strategies over 146 walk-forward OOS folds and conditions results on realized-volatility and momentum states. Its methodological message is directly relevant: an unconditional full-horizon statistic can hide economically important regime dependence. A strategy that is excellent in trends and destructive in high-volatility chop should not be described merely by its average Sharpe.

V8 therefore treats regime cells as a vulnerability topology. Regime labels themselves remain measurement constructs: they must be causal, versioned, and defined before protected results. The benchmark reports both cell values and coverage. It is forbidden to assign a favorable regime label after seeing the outcome.

MinervaScore: the crucial precedent for score + hard gates

LITERATURE_SUPPORTED MinervaScore (August 2026) is unusually close to the design tension in V8. It combines five robustness/statistical components—including Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Probability of Backtest Overfitting, SPA, minimum track-record considerations and regime stability—into a raw score mapped to 0–100, while reserving high certification bands for strategies that clear all required gates. Its authors explicitly warn that the score is not a probability of future profitability.

The paper is also epistemically useful because its own preregistered unseen real-market test did not produce a significant relationship between the score and forward returns. Rather than hiding that result, the proposed use is validation/reporting and triage. This is exactly the distinction D-153 needs: a scalar can summarize evidence quality and robustness diagnostics without becoming an expected-return estimator.

DiagnosticScore ≠ Readiness ≠ P(Future Profit)

V8 should not copy MinervaScore weights or thresholds. The paper is recent, and one study does not establish an industry standard. Its value is as a concrete demonstration that score and gate can coexist without gate compensation.

Source: “MinervaScore: A Composite Robustness Score for Backtested Trading Strategies,” arXiv:2608.23808.

“Honest evaluation”: why statistical sophistication cannot repair leakage

A 2026 study of LLM-driven strategy discovery separates structural no-lookahead controls from search-aware statistical correction and records every evaluated strategy as research debt. Its most important demonstration is adversarial: a deliberately leaky oracle can achieve an absurdly high Sharpe and can still survive statistical corrections such as DSR/PBO. The conclusion is not that those statistics are useless; it is that their null models do not repair a violated causal graph.

This validates a core V8 ordering rule:

G0/G1: identity + PIT/causal integrity
          ↓
G2: deterministic/ledger integrity
          ↓
G3+: statistical/economic evaluation

If causality fails:
    stop.
Do not ask a p-value to save the experiment.

The same logic applies to evaluator gaming. If an agent sees hidden benchmark answers, optimization has contaminated the test. A more conservative confidence interval cannot unsee that information. Therefore structural gates precede statistical gates.

Source: “What survives honest evaluation? Leakage-safe, search-aware assessment of LLM-driven trading strategy discovery,” arXiv:2608.27734.

Synthetic financial research: useful wind tunnels, weak probability authority

Recent financial time-series generation research emphasizes controllability, stylized facts, multivariate dependence, factor structure and usability under downstream tasks. Market-GAN explicitly evaluates alignment, fidelity and usability; newer GAN/diffusion work targets stylized facts and inter-asset correlation; multivariate generators increasingly test tail co-movement and factor structure rather than only marginal return histograms.

This supports the Foundry's insistence that generator realism is a vector. A generator can match marginal returns while failing volatility clustering, stress correlation, funding dynamics or the very V8 behavior occupancy used by the policy. Passing one “realism score” is therefore insufficient.

The epistemic boundary remains strict. Synthetic worlds are models. Their scenario frequencies are generated frequencies. Unless a separate, validated probabilistic environment model establishes calibration, “20% of synthetic worlds lost money” is not “20% probability of losing money next year.” Synthetic data is strongest as coverage and counterexample machinery.

Sources: Market-GAN, arXiv:2309.07708; GAN-Diffusion synthetic financial time series, arXiv:2605.27113; MarketGANs, arXiv:2601.17773.

Execution research: historical replay, reference engines and interactive simulators

Execution evaluation has a fidelity ladder. Bar-based replay can be adequate when conclusions are insensitive to intrabar ordering and market impact. A more detailed reference engine can expose accounting, fill, fee, buying-power or order-semantics disagreements. Interactive agent-based simulators such as ABIDES become relevant only when the policy's own orders plausibly alter the environment or queue/latency semantics determine the result.

LEAN is attractive as an independent referee because its “reality modeling” layer explicitly separates fill, slippage, fee, brokerage, buying-power, settlement and capacity assumptions and supports local Docker backtests with machine-readable results. But LEAN is not ground truth. A default slippage or fill model can be wrong for a particular crypto venue; market impact is difficult; custom modeling may be required. The correct V8 use is paired disagreement testing, not outsourcing epistemology.

Build-vs-buy rule

A component should be internal only when at least one of these holds: its semantics are unique to V8; external packages cannot preserve the required authority/data-role boundary; an external dependency prevents deterministic/reproducible evaluation; or independent implementation is itself needed as a parity oracle. This reverses a common engineering instinct. The burned historical court: keep it, cap it, exploit it for diagnosis

The 12-month quad should not be deleted. Its correct role is analogous to a regression fixture with rich economic telemetry. It answers questions that a hidden OOS should not be repeatedly opened to answer: which Expert emits too many candidates, which symbol carries the result, where fees dominate, which exit truncates MFE, whether capital quantization creates zero-size orders, and which Oracle gap is largest.

D-153 therefore gives BURNED_HISTORICAL two uses:

  1. Pathology bench: dense attribution and ablation are allowed because evidence authority is already burned.
  2. Regression sentinel: an implementation change that unexpectedly destroys or inflates known behavior is immediately visible.

It is explicitly not the dominant score source. A system can improve +20 points on the burned tape while getting worse on sealed qualification worlds. That is exactly the pattern the benchmark must expose.

Protected frozen OOS: the economic replication court

Protected frozen OOS is expensive evidence because it is consumable. A policy must be frozen before the segment is opened; benchmark primary metrics and gates must also be frozen; no repair is allowed after seeing the result without creating a challenger and paying for a new protected case.

The protected court should be broad enough to test the intended deployment envelope, not chosen because it flatters a symbol or period. For V8's selective architecture, broad universe evaluation does not mean forced equal allocation. The selector may legally allocate 100% of authorized risk to one opportunity or 0% to all opportunities if that is what the frozen policy does.

Protected OOS report minimums

  • after-cost geometric growth and terminal equity;
  • drawdown, expected shortfall/tail metrics, time under water;
  • trade/campaign count and effective episode count;
  • symbol and factor concentration;
  • Oracle discovery/value transfer diagnostics;
  • friction retention and cost sensitivity;
  • paired incumbent/challenger differences;
  • statistical-plan receipts and multiplicity state;
  • coverage and missingness;
  • explicit burn receipt.

Walk-forward evaluation

Walk-forward evaluation approximates repeated deployment decisions more faithfully than a single train/test split. At each fold, training and optional validation use only the historical prefix available at that time; the evaluation window is strictly later. For V8, the policy may be re-estimated only if the experiment is explicitly about a learning procedure. If the subject is one frozen production policy, the policy remains frozen and walk-forward windows simply provide multiple chronological observation cells.

Reverse stress: nearest plausible defeater as a primary benchmark product

Ordinary stress asks what happens under named scenarios. Reverse stress starts with a failure and searches backward for a world that causes it. This makes failure proximity measurable.

d* = minθ Distance(θ, admissible-manifold)   subject to   Failure(P, θ)=true

The word “plausible” is dangerous unless operationalized. D-153 therefore requires the distance function, admissible support, generator passport and search algorithm to be part of the receipt. A small d* means a failure exists close to the declared world manifold; it does not mean that failure has high future probability.

Anti-gaming constraint

Concentration audit

A result carried by one asset is not automatically invalid. It is, however, more fragile if the asset was selected after observing its outcome. The benchmark distinguishes:

  • ex-ante concentration: legal if produced by frozen selector/allocator;
  • ex-post concentration: selection bias if the winning asset was chosen after seeing performance;
  • single-mechanism dependency: diagnostic vulnerability even when ex-ante legal;
  • universe expansion invariance: adding weak assets must not mechanically increase risk/trade count.

CapabilityScore: a diagnostic scalar without scalar readiness

D-153 permits one scalar because Kaizen and humans need a compact regression signal. The score is deliberately constructed from domain margins, not arbitrary raw metric averages, and is valid only within one BenchmarkVersion.

Step 1 — metric margin

For a metric where higher is better, define preregistered unacceptable L and strong U reference points:

m(x) = clip((x − L)/(U − L), 0, 1)

For lower-is-better metrics the transform is reversed. Thresholds are measurement references, not metaphysical boundaries. If a metric has a hard safety limit, breach is separately represented in GateVector; clipping it to zero does not make the breach compensable.

Step 2 — domain score

Dk = 100 × RobustAggregate({mkj}, metric weights, coverage, dependence)

The default robust aggregate is a preregistered weighted trimmed/geometric construction that penalizes a near-zero critical submetric more than an arithmetic mean. Exact function is a benchmark-version decision and must be validated for ranking stability.

Step 3 — total diagnostic score

CapabilityScore = Σ wkDk,   Σwk=1

Anti-gaming design: preventing benchmark overfit from replacing backtest overfit

Once a benchmark is optimized repeatedly, it ceases to be a passive judge. D-153 therefore treats evaluator capture as an expected failure mode rather than a remote possibility.

Five firewalls

  1. Public DEV / hidden qualification split. Policy agents may optimize against DEV batteries but cannot see qualification seeds, protected OOS outcomes or scorer internals that would invalidate the test.
  2. Novelty refresh. New synthetic/adversarial families can be added as versioned evaluator evidence after policy freeze. Historical passes remain truthful for their old coverage but do not automatically cover the new world space.
  3. Weight sensitivity. Challenger rank is recomputed across a preregistered neighborhood of defensible score weights. High rank reversal downgrades the interpretation.
  4. Metric mutation tests. Introduce policies that deliberately exploit a metric proxy—e.g., never trade to improve precision, or trade continuously to improve recall—and require the benchmark to reject the gaming strategy.
  5. Benchmark trial debt. Repeated benchmark exposures are logged. If an agent has effectively trained on the qualification battery, that battery's role is burned for that lineage.

Score saturation

If mature policies cluster near 95–100, the benchmark has lost discriminatory power. D-153 requires periodic item analysis: domain variance, correlation between metrics, difficulty distribution, failure discovery rate and incremental information. Metrics that are always passed may remain constitutional invariants but should not consume large diagnostic score weight.

Projection aggregation: from heterogeneous evidence to a capital distribution

The projection engine must resist a seductive but invalid shortcut: mix every historical, CPCV and synthetic path into one bag and call its histogram the future distribution. Different sources carry different sampling semantics. D-153 therefore separates scenario distribution from probability-calibrated projection.

Mode 1 — Empirical scenario distribution

Used at Grade D/C when probability calibration is weak. Report quantiles of declared cases with language such as “among these evaluation cases.” No claim is made that each case is equally probable in the future.

Mode 2 — Regime-conditioned empirical mixture

When real protected/prospective data support causal regime frequencies, the ProjectionPlan may weight real OOS cases by a versioned regime model. The regime assignment uses only decision-time information. Weight uncertainty is propagated rather than fixed silently.

F(y) = Σr πr F(y | r, contract, policy)

Here π_r is not guessed from synthetic frequency; it requires an empirical/calibrated source and carries uncertainty.

Mode 3 — Bootstrap / block-resampled future-equity trajectories

Block or campaign-level resampling may estimate compounding uncertainty when stationarity assumptions are defensible. The block unit must respect dependence and campaign duration. Outputs are tagged model-derived.

Mode 4 — Bayesian/model ensemble (future extension)

A future ProjectionPlan may combine regime-conditioned empirical evidence, parameter uncertainty and model uncertainty through an explicit Bayesian or ensemble model. This is a separate research object and cannot be smuggled in under the label “Monte Carlo.”

Synthetic role

Synthetic worlds contribute stress bounds, tail scenarios and failure topology. Unless a generator is separately calibrated as a probabilistic environment model, its case frequency has zero direct weight in the expected-return mixture. A synthetic catastrophe can widen the risk narrative or defeat robustness without pretending to know its frequency.

Every projection and benchmark comparison includes an update rule. This prevents the report from presenting uncertainty as decorative error bars.

Provisional promotion rule

PROMOTION_CANDIDATE if and only if:

    no hard gate regresses
    AND no protected critical domain crosses its floor
    AND total diagnostic improvement ≥ preregistered minimum_effect
    AND at least one intended causal domain improves
    AND paired protected evidence supports the change where required
    AND multiplicity/statistical debt is paid
    AND benchmark access policy was not violated

A challenger can be accepted for engineering improvement even if economic authority remains unchanged. Example: an execution-adapter fix may raise parity and reliability while leaving the policy's edge claim at NO_ECONOMIC_CLAIM. Kaizen decisions therefore specify the kind of improvement, not merely “better/worse.”

Failure-first research queue

Instead of sorting work solely by score gradient, Kaizen prioritizes bottlenecks:

  1. hard defeaters;
  2. blocked gates that prevent the next authority level;
  3. nearest catastrophic failure surfaces;
  4. largest Target/actual recoverability loss with decision-time support;
  5. largest execution/friction conversion loss;
  6. high-uncertainty/high-information benchmark gaps;
  7. only then broad score improvements.
