This is a very simple DCF model—the one we use at Morningstar has
about a dozen Excel tabs, adjusts for complicated items such as pensions and
operating leases, and explicitly models competitive advantage periods, among
many other things. But a model doesn’t need to be super complex to get you
most of the way there and help you clarify your thinking. For example, our
valuation of Clorox—which pegs the value of the stock at about 15 percent
higher than the stock price in late 2003—has the company generating free
cash flow of about $800 million per year 10 years from now.
